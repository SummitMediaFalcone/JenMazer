# Local SEO Plan — Jenifer Mazer, West Palm Beach

Research completed July 2026. Market: Palm Beach County primary, Treasure Coast
north, Broward/Miami-Dade south.

**Data honesty note:** there are no verified search-volume numbers in here.
Paid keyword tools sit behind logins, and Google Trends returned nothing at
metro level for these terms. Competitiveness calls are reasoned estimates from
SERP composition and competitor density, marked `[EST]`. What *is* verified and
cited: who currently ranks, what those sites do and don't do, GBP category
names, Florida cottage food law, and census data.

## 🚨 Read this first — a legal problem with the pastry line

Florida cottage food law lets her sell cookies, cakes, cupcakes and brownies
from a home kitchen with no license up to $250K/year. It **prohibits cream and
custard fillings and anything needing refrigeration.**

That means **cannoli and cream puffs are almost certainly outside cottage
food** — and under a strict reading, so is buttercream.

Right now the site features the cannoli-and-cream-puff platter as the hero
image of the pastries service card, lists "Cannoli & cream puffs" as a checkbox
in the lead form, and shows them in the gallery. **Nothing is published yet, so
there is no live exposure** — but this has to be resolved before launch.

Three ways out, her call:
1. She already produces those in a licensed/shared commercial kitchen → nothing
   changes, we just confirm it.
2. She moves that production to a licensed kitchen → nothing changes.
3. She can't → we pull cannoli and cream puffs from the site and lead with
   cookies, meringues, chocolate-dipped pretzels and dry-decorated items, which
   are unambiguously fine.

Do not build SEO pages targeting "cannoli" or "cream puffs" until this is
settled. Sources: [Florida Cottage Food Laws](https://cakerybakeries.com/cottage-laws/florida),
[Cottage Food License — FL](https://www.cottagefoodlicense.com/state/florida).

## The positioning that wins

Every competitor in Palm Beach County is a planner **or** a baker **or** a
balloon company. Verified across a dozen sites — none of them bundle.

> **One person makes your whole party — decor, desserts, and the hand-painted sign.**

No competitor can copy that without hiring. Build the brand on it.

## 2. Which city pages to build

Google's 2026 helpful-content classifier flags near-duplicate location pages as
doorway pages and deindexes them. A solo operator publishing 40 templated city
pages gets penalised, not ranked.

**Rule of thumb: one city page per city she'd genuinely drive to on a Saturday
with a car full of desserts and a six-foot banner.** That's about eight, not forty.

**Build first (months 1–3)**
1. Party design — West Palm Beach *(home base, GBP anchor)*
2. Custom cookies & desserts — West Palm Beach *(best competition-to-intent ratio)*
3. Baby showers & gender reveals — West Palm Beach
4. Party design — Lake Worth Beach / Lantana *(10–15 min, denser, more price-sensitive — the right budget audience)*
5. Party design — Wellington / Royal Palm Beach *(highest kids'-birthday density in the county, no real competitor page)*
6. Hand-painted banners & signs *(national, not city-scoped — fastest win available)*

**Build second (months 4–8)** — Delray/Boynton, Palm Beach Gardens/Jupiter,
then Port St. Lucie *(highest upside per effort, but ~55 min each way — only
worth it with a travel minimum or delivery-only dessert offer)*.

**Skip** — Boca Raton (saturated, expects luxury positioning she isn't selling),
Fort Lauderdale (60–75 min, entrenched vendors), **Miami (75–90 min, deepest
vendor bench in the state — do not build a page)**. Keep them as "will travel
for larger events" copy, not as ranking targets.

## 4. The "cheap party planning" question — don't do it

Richard asked to target "cheap party planning" and "budget party planning".
I checked those SERPs directly. **They return Amazon, Dollar Tree, Factory
Direct Party and DIY listicles — not a single service provider.**

That's Google stating the intent plainly: people searching "cheap party" are
trying to *avoid hiring someone*. Ranking for it brings traffic that won't
convert, plus quote requests from people with $80 for a full setup. For a solo
operator whose scarcest asset is hours, not clicks, that's the worst outcome.

Second cost: the word "cheap" on her own site anchors her price downward before
she quotes. She's competing against operators who publish **no pricing at all** —
four sites checked, four with nothing. Being the cheap one isn't a position.
Being the *knowable* one is.

**Two groups of price-shoppers, only one worth having:**

- **Group A — DIY intent (avoid):** "cheap party decorations", "budget birthday
  party ideas", "diy dessert table". Retail and blog SERPs. No service pages here.
- **Group B — real buyers with a ceiling (capture these):** "affordable baby
  shower packages west palm beach", "how much does a dessert table cost", "small
  birthday party package", "party planner under $500". These people have already
  decided to hire someone. They're just afraid of a $5,000 minimum — reasonably,
  since a WPB competitor publishes exactly that. Group B converts well because
  their fear is specific and one sentence dissolves it.

**What to do instead:**
1. **Publish a real Pricing & Packages page.** No verified competitor has one.
   It captures every "how much / cost / prices" query in the county and
   pre-qualifies inquiries before they reach her inbox.
2. **Use "affordable", "no minimums", "packages that start small" — never
   "cheap".** Same budget-conscious searcher, opposite buyer.
3. **Lead with "no minimum."** Her real edge is taking the $400 baby shower that
   the $5K-minimum shops won't return a call about. That isn't being cheap,
   it's being *available*: "No $5,000 minimum. No package you have to grow into."
4. **Frame price against DIY labour, not other planners.** "You're paying for my
   hands, not an agency's overhead."
5. **If the word "cheap" must appear, confine it to one blog post** — "Planning a
   Beautiful Party on a Budget in West Palm Beach" — with an honest DIY guide and
   a soft CTA. Never a service page, never a title tag.

## 5. Competitors

**Luxury planners** — Events by Mala (best local SEO found: real city×service
pages, ~1,200 words, FAQ blocks — but "bespoke" positioning and diluted across
six countries). Silver Lining Occasions (florals + balloons, no desserts, no
signage). Elite Events ($5K minimum).

**Balloon specialists** — Sparkle Events, A Beautiful Occasion, Fashion
Balloons, My Balloon World. They own "party decorations WPB". Balloons only.

**Bakers** — Frosted by Marissa is her closest true competitor: home-based,
Palm Beach County, hand-frosted cookies, corporate logo cookies, well
positioned, no pricing shown. Atlantic Cookie is a Weebly photo gallery with no
pricing, turnaround times or minimums.

**Five verified gaps she can win:**
1. **Nobody bundles design + desserts + signage.** The whole strategy.
2. **Zero pricing transparency market-wide.** First mover takes the query cluster.
3. **Hand-painted banners have no local competitor.** Only Etsy — and a local
   maker with same-week turnaround and hand delivery beats 4-week shipping.
4. **Content is shallow.** Nobody publishes minimums, turnaround, allergens,
   flavours, delivery radius. Answering what everyone dodges *is* the strategy.
5. **Gender reveal is Miami-concentrated.** Palm Beach coverage is thin, demand
   is urgent, the date is non-negotiable.

**Honest risk:** Frosted by Marissa occupies the same home-based niche and
executes well. Don't try to beat her on cookies — beat her on **scope**. Jen is
the one person who also styles the table and paints the sign.

## 6. Google Business Profile

- **Primary category: Party planner.** Not Bakery or Cake shop — primary
  category is the heaviest local ranking factor, and bakery categories push
  Google toward storefront expectations a home-based operator can't meet.
- **Secondary (pick 5, not 9 — overstuffing dilutes):** Event planner, Cake
  shop, Caterer *(only if licensing supports it)*, Banner store, Bakery.
- **Hide the street address.** Required for a genuine service-area business and
  it does **not** hurt rankings — Google keeps the address internally for
  distance maths.
- **Define areas by named city, not radius.** ~10–12 cities, not the full 20 —
  there's no bonus for filling every slot and an overstuffed area reads as less
  specific.
- **Non-negotiable:** 25+ original photos (never stock), weekly uploads,
  Products with prices, Services filled out, a weekly Post, and a written
  review request after every single event.

## 8. First 30 days, ranked by return per hour

1. **Google Business Profile, fully built (days 1–3).** For a new local business
   this outranks the website as a lead source for the first six months or more.
   Nothing else on this list is close.
2. **Publish the Pricing & Packages page (week 1).** No competitor has one.
   Highest-converting page she'll own.
3. **Ship the hand-painted banner page (weeks 1–2).** Only Etsy competes.
   Rankable in weeks, ships nationally, and it's the clearest proof of the
   "made by one pair of hands" story. Feed the images to Pinterest too.
4. **Get 10 Google reviews (weeks 1–4).** She starts at zero against competitors
   with 15–19. Ask every client in writing within 24 hours of the event.
5. **Publish the two WPB money pages (weeks 2–4)** — Custom Cookies and Baby
   Showers, 800–1,200 words each, with the minimums, turnaround, flavours,
   allergens, delivery radius and starting prices that competitors omit.

**Explicitly NOT in the first 30 days:** link building, 40 city pages, chasing
"party planner west palm beach" to page one, or anything targeting "cheap".
And resolve the cannoli/cream-puff licensing question before writing a word of
copy about them.
